title: Glossary
description: "Plain-language definitions for the terms used throughout the NJ PBS editorial workflow."
---

## Boardwalk parts

### Boardwalk

The staff product at admin.njpbs.org. The address and Admin settings keep the word Admin.

### Rail

The side navigation in Boardwalk. It is not a home-page shelf, and it is not the public header or footer.

### Destination

A built-in rail entry, such as Home, Pages, or Media library. A destination is not a plank.

### Plank

One link a staff member adds to their rail.

### Shared plank

A plank the owner has shared. Another staff member can turn it on to add it to their own rail.

### Span

A named group of planks in the rail.

### Area

Watch, Support, Station or Compliance. An Area groups content for staff. Accounts
with access levels use site-wide capabilities; existing area grants can still
limit access to named areas.

### Workspace

One destination's working screen, such as Pages or the editorial queue.

## Editorial records

### All content

The combined library of pages, posts, and other authorized record types
available to your account. It supports search, filters, personal views, and
result paging.

### Assignment

The person or group currently responsible for the next editorial action on a
record.

### Block

One ordered unit of page or post content, such as prose, a heading, image,
notice, card group, or service-specific configuration.

### Block palette

The set of block types approved for a particular page template or record type.

### Canonical page

A fixed NJ PBS public page whose identity and path come from the shared route
registry.

### Content type

The kind of record being edited, such as a page or post. Content type controls
available fields, blocks, routing, and workflow.

### Derived content

Public content supplied by a shared feed, service, or adapter rather than copied
into the CMS draft.

### Draft

The latest version stored in the CMS for editorial work. A saved draft is not
necessarily public.

### Locked region

Public page context shown in the editor but intentionally not editable as an
ordinary block, often because an integration or fixed shell owns it.

### Post

An individual editorial record with its own title, metadata, body, route
identity, and lifecycle.

### Public path

The route where approved content appears on the public website. It is often
read-only in the editor.

### Record

A general term for a saved CMS item, including pages, posts, media assets, and
profile data.

### Slug

The route-friendly text used in a post URL. The CMS protects established route
identity and can preserve prior slugs for redirects.

## Saving and recovery

### Baseline

The stored record version that the editor loaded before you began changing the
working copy.

### Editor lock

A courtesy reservation showing that another authorized person is actively
editing a record.

### Revision

A stored version and its evidence: who saved it, when, and what changed.

### Revision history

The ordered record of saved versions available for inspection and, for
authorized roles, restoration.

### Restore

An action that makes an earlier revision the new current draft while retaining
evidence of the recovery.

### Unsaved work

A browser working copy that differs from the stored draft.

### Version conflict

A warning that the stored draft changed after the current browser loaded its
baseline.

### Working copy

The fields and blocks currently shown in your browser, including changes that
may not yet be saved.

## Review and publication

### Approval

An authorized editorial decision that a saved draft may proceed to its next
lifecycle stage.

### Editorial queue

The workspace for submitted items, review tasks, and publication activity
available to your role.

### Lifecycle

The record's editorial and public state, such as draft, submitted, scheduled,
published, or archived.

### Preview

A protected rendered view of a specific saved draft. It does not change the
public site.

### Publish

The authorized action that begins releasing an approved saved version to the
public site.

### Schedule

An instruction to release approved content at a specified date and time.

### Submit

The action that moves a saved draft to editorial review.

### Unpublish

An authorized action that removes or replaces a public version according to the
product rules. It is not the same as deleting the record.

### Verify live

Check the public URL after release and confirm that the intended version,
assets, links, and layout are present.

## Metadata and media

### Alternative text

A contextual text equivalent for the meaningful information in an image.

### Caption

Editorial context displayed with an image or media item.

### Credit

The required attribution for the creator or source of an asset.

### Crop position

The important position in an image that responsive crops should preserve. The control is labeled Crop position.

### Media asset

A reusable station-managed image, document, video or audio file stored in the media
library.

### Metadata

Structured information about a record, including title, description, author,
dates, search fields, and social-sharing fields.

### Search description

A concise summary intended for search results and discovery.

### Social image

The image selected for a link preview on supported sharing platforms.

### Social title and description

Metadata written for a shared-link context while remaining faithful to the
published content.

### Category

A media filing label, such as General, Entertainment or Staff profiles. Pickers
show each category's description. Categories are separate from areas; media
permissions still depend on the stored category.

### Origin

Where a media item came from: Site build, Uploaded or Voiceover. Origin appears
separately from categories. Source still names a video library in Boardwalk.

## People and permissions

### Account identity

The verified email and sign-in identity used to attribute CMS actions.

### Manager

Can also delete content, manage the schedule and settings, and manage staff
accounts. Cannot grant or revoke roles.

### Capability

One specific authorized action, such as editing content, managing media,
viewing activity, or granting roles.

### Viewer

Can read the private guide, browse authorized audio, file problem reports and read
their own reports. Cannot change content or manage media, settings or staff access.

### Writer

Can draft and edit their own work in their areas. An Editor publishes it. Cannot
edit other people's work or manage media, settings or staff access.

### Editor

Can edit and publish any work in their areas, and manage media in authorized
categories. Cannot delete content or manage the schedule, settings or staff
access.

### Least privilege

Grant only the access required for the current work.

### Role

A named bundle of capabilities.

### Area grant

An access limit tied to named areas for an account that still uses role and area
grants. Accounts with an access level use that level's site-wide capabilities.

### Site owner

Can do everything available in Boardwalk, including give access. Cannot change
their own administrative role, revoke their own roles or remove the last Site
owner.

## Administration and evidence

### Activity

The administrative audit view of content, lifecycle, profile, and access
events available to authorized roles.

### Admin settings

Global newsroom configuration that can affect many users or public surfaces.

### Account settings

Personal display and navigation preferences that do not change public content
or permissions.

### Exact error text

The complete message shown by the CMS, copied without paraphrase or sensitive
credentials.

### Request identifier

A value that ties a preview, publication, or other asynchronous operation to
its status and logs.

### Terminal status

The final success or failure state of an asynchronous operation, as opposed to
queued or running.

## Access and security

### Access request

A verified request for an administrator to approve an identity and assign the
appropriate role.

### Authentication

Proof of account identity, such as Google sign-in or email and password.

### Authorization

The capabilities the CMS permits after identity has been established.

### Verification link

A credential-bearing one-time link used to prove control of an email address.
It must not be shared.

### Sensitive information

Passwords, tokens, member or donor data, unpublished agreements, private
personnel information, or other material that must not appear in public content
or ordinary support reports.
