Open **Profile** from the account menu to prepare the public information used
for your byline or staff page.

Fields can include:

- Display name.
- Public title or role.
- Biography.
- Profile photograph and crop.
- Public contact or social links when approved.
- Publication status.

Use the name and description NJ PBS is authorized to publish. Do not place
private phone numbers, personal addresses, emergency contacts, or internal job
notes in a public profile field.

After saving:

1. Wait for confirmation.
2. Reload the profile.
3. Check the photograph and crop.
4. Preview or open the public profile when the workflow permits it.
5. Correct outdated public information promptly.

When profile publishing is on, Editors, Publishers, Admins and Owners can use
**Publish profile** for their own saved profile. Contributors can save and
unpublish their profile. Ask an admin to publish it from **Staff profiles**.
Admins and Owners can publish other staff profiles there. Viewers cannot
publish author profiles.

Upload a photo from the profile, or choose any image from the media library.
If the image has no profile-photo rights record, the CMS shows a warning and
asks how NJ PBS may use it. Your answer is saved with the profile. If the file
is already in the media library, the CMS uses that copy.

A useful profile image:

- Clearly identifies the person.
- Has adequate resolution.
- Uses a crop that works at small avatar size and on the public profile.
- Includes accurate credit or rights information when required.
- Does not reveal private information in the background.

Set the focal point so the person's face remains visible across responsive
crops. Check both the account-menu avatar and the public profile treatment.

## Review Staff profiles

Managers and designated profile managers can use **Staff profiles** to
review, correct, withdraw, or republish public-facing staff information.

Before changing another person's profile:

- Confirm the identity.
- Confirm the request or source of the correction.
- Distinguish account identity from public byline data.
- Preserve the person's approved name and biography.
- Record why a profile was withdrawn or corrected.
- Check where the byline is already used.

Withdrawing a public profile should not silently delete the staff access record,
and removing CMS access should not automatically erase valid historical
bylines. Treat the two systems as related but distinct.

**Staff access** shows approved identities, roles, status, and area grants
available to your administrative role.

Use it to answer:

- Is the account verified and active?
- Which role does it hold?
- Is access site-wide or limited to an area?
- When was the role last changed?
- Who approved the change?
- Does the account still have a current business need?

Only a Site owner can grant or revoke roles, approve account requests or deactivate
staff accounts. Managers can view staff accounts and manage staff profiles, but
cannot give access, approve account requests or deactivate staff accounts.

## Apply least privilege

When a Site owner changes access:

1. Confirm the person's identity.
2. Confirm the work they need to perform.
3. Choose the narrowest role and area grant that supports it.
4. Avoid temporary Manager access when an Editor grant is sufficient.
5. Record the reason and expected duration.
6. Ask the person to sign out and back in.
7. Verify one expected destination and one expected restriction.
8. Remove or narrow access when the work ends.

Do not share accounts. Each person needs an individual identity so revision and
activity evidence remains meaningful.

**Activity** is the administrative audit view for content and access changes.

Search or filter by:

- Person or account email.
- Record.
- Action.
- Content type.
- Access or lifecycle event.

Use the chevron beside an activity heading to inspect its details. Use the record's own revision
history for content-level comparison and Activity for the wider administrative
sequence.

Activity evidence helps investigate what happened. It should not be used to
publicly evaluate staff performance without the appropriate context and policy.

## Investigate a change

For a disputed or unexpected action:

1. Record the public or CMS symptom.
2. Identify the record and approximate time.
3. Open revision history.
4. Open Activity and filter to the record or person.
5. Establish the sequence of save, lifecycle, access, and release actions.
6. Check whether another editor or automated process acted between them.
7. Correct the current state.
8. Preserve the evidence and document the operational conclusion.

Do not edit activity records or screenshots to make the sequence appear simpler.

**Admin settings** contains global configuration available to authorized
Managers. A setting can affect many users or public pages even when the
control itself looks small.

Global settings can include newsroom-wide presentation or communication
options, such as a banner.

Before changing a global setting:

- Identify every surface it affects.
- Copy the current approved value.
- Confirm the start and end conditions.
- Check text length, links, and accessibility.
- Arrange a second review for high-visibility changes.
- Know how to restore the previous state.

After saving, verify the intended Admin and public surfaces. Remove temporary
messages on schedule.

## Maintain Account settings

**Account settings** changes personal CMS presentation rather than public
content.

Settings can include:

- Text-label or icon control presentation on wider screens. Phones always show
  icons.
- Navigation arrangement and visibility.
- Density preferences.
- Other display choices identified as personal.

A personal preference does not change permissions. Reset the navigation
arrangement when a new eligible destination is difficult to find.

## Protect sensitive administration

Never include the following in a profile, setting, activity note, screenshot, or
support report:

- Passwords or reset links.
- Session tokens.
- Cloud or service credentials.
- Private member or donor data.
- Unpublished agreements.
- Private personnel information.
- A full access list when only one account is relevant.

Redact unrelated identities and browser data from screenshots. Use the
approved private security channel for suspected credential or authorization
problems.

## Complete an administrative handoff

A useful handoff states:

- The exact account, record, or setting involved.
- The prior and current state.
- The reason for the change.
- The approver.
- The time and time zone.
- Any required sign-out or verification step.
- The public or operational check completed.
- The next owner.

Avoid vague statements such as “access fixed” or “banner updated.” State what
was verified.
