# 2026 NJ PBS operator selection

New Jersey selected [Montclair State University](montclair-ccm.md) to operate
[NJ PBS](nj-pbs.md) on June 3, 2026. The university was one of four bidders in
a competitive process led by the state treasurer. The announcement identified
Montclair's College of Communication and Media as the network's new home.
[New Jersey Treasury announcement](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/news/2026/06032026.shtml)

## Background and request for proposals

The selection followed the approaching expiration of Public Media NJ's 2011
operating agreement on June 30, 2026. The state's February 5 request for
proposals sought a successor arrangement to maintain public television service.
It covered the four main stations and three television translators, which extend
broadcast coverage. The state favored a single programming and management
agreement, while also accepting proposals for more limited services.
[State request for proposals, sections 1 and 2](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/njpba/assets/files/pdf/NJPBA-RFP.pdf)

The request placed the process within the framework of the 2010 New Jersey
Public Broadcasting System Transfer Act. As the document explained, the act
allowed contracts for station operation while retaining the state's television
licenses. Its public-service priorities included New Jersey news and public
affairs, coverage of major state addresses and elections, and reaching audiences
on digital platforms. These requirements connected the search for an operator
to the stations' continuing statewide educational purpose.
[State request for proposals, sections 1–3](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/njpba/assets/files/pdf/NJPBA-RFP.pdf)

The state published answers to prospective bidders' questions on February 19.
These became part of the request for proposals. Among the clarifications, the
state said additional program streams remained subject to federal requirements
for noncommercial educational television.
[Public bidder questions and answers](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/njpba/assets/files/pdf/NJPBA-RFP-QA-Responses.pdf)

## Selection and public commitments

The June announcement described a five-year agreement with two possible
five-year extensions and a 15-day legislative review period. Montclair committed
to at least six hours of New Jersey programming each week, including a weekday
newscast and public affairs coverage. The proposal also described student
learning opportunities and reporting partnerships through the Center for
Cooperative Media. Announced accountability measures included journalism ethics,
an independent ethics committee with outside members, and a community advisory
board. These were commitments presented with the selection.
[Treasury announcement](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/news/2026/06032026.shtml)

In a June 16 interview later broadcast by *State of Affairs with Steve Adubato*,
dean Keith Strudler described the university's ambitions for the service. He
linked the station's campus setting to experimentation in public media and to
opportunities for students. The interview records the university's
public explanation of its approach during the period between selection and the
start of its agreement.
[PBS interview, broadcast July 11, 2026](https://www.pbs.org/video/asm-al-barlas-r-nj-keith-strudler-phd-nco7mf/)

## Agreement takes effect

NJ PBS confirmed Montclair's start as operator in its July 1 newsletter, which
described the following three months as a period of preparation for new
programming. The [later transition](operator-transition.md) included a separate
timetable for the new daily newscast.
[NJ PBS transition newsletter](https://mailchi.mp/67a85f3eee1d/a-new-chapter-for-nj-pbs)
