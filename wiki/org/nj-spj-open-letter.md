# NJ-SPJ open letter about the NJ PBS transition

The board of the New Jersey Society of Professional Journalists published an
open letter to Keith Strudler, dean of Montclair's
[College of Communication and Media](montclair-ccm.md), on June 24, 2026. It
addressed the university's forthcoming management of [NJ PBS](nj-pbs.md).
The chapter published both the text and a downloadable PDF on its website.
[NJ-SPJ open letter](https://www.njspj.org/montclair-nj-pbs-open-letter/)

## Context and recommendations

The board acknowledged concerns about university management of a public
broadcaster while noting that the model existed elsewhere in the United States.
It cited Wisconsin and North Carolina among its examples. The chapter said it
was not endorsing an operator; its expectations applied to journalism throughout
New Jersey.
[NJ-SPJ letter](https://www.njspj.org/montclair-nj-pbs-open-letter/)

Its first recommendation was a clear separation between newsroom decisions,
station management, and university administration. The second was adherence to
SPJ's ethics code, supported by public media integrity guidance. The third was
regular attention to all 21 counties. The board connected that request to New
Jersey's position between two large media markets and the uneven availability
of local reporting. The letter framed these recommendations as a means of
improving community coverage and maintaining audience trust.
[NJ-SPJ letter](https://www.njspj.org/montclair-nj-pbs-open-letter/)

## Ethical standards referenced

SPJ's code organizes its guidance around accurate reporting, reducing harm,
independence, and public accountability. It asks journalists to verify facts,
provide context, disclose unavoidable conflicts, explain decisions, and correct
errors. SPJ describes the code as professional guidance rather than an
enforceable law. This distinction helps explain the letter's emphasis on
newsroom practice and responsibility to viewers.
[SPJ code of ethics](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/)

The other resource, *Editorial Integrity for Public Media*, grew from a joint
effort by the Station Resource Group and public television's Affinity Group
Coalition. It provides model guidance for public broadcasters. Its resources
address editorial partnerships, transparency in choosing stories, and the
boundaries needed to protect editorial decisions. The letter directed the new
operator to this wider body of guidance alongside SPJ's code.
