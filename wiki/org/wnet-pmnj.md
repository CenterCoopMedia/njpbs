# WNET / Public Media NJ

The WNET Group is a nonprofit public media institution whose subsidiary, Public Media NJ Inc. (PMNJ), operated [NJ PBS](nj-pbs.md) from 2011 through June 30, 2026. [WNET's public transition notice](https://thirteen.org/serving-new-jersey-into-the-future/).

The parent organization and its New Jersey subsidiary had distinct roles. WNET brought a wider broadcasting and production organization to the arrangement; PMNJ was the operator under an agreement with the New Jersey Public Broadcasting Authority. The state retained the television stations. [American Archive of Public Broadcasting history](https://americanarchive.org/participating-orgs/1809), [NJPBA station information](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/njpba/).

## The wider WNET organization

WNET adopted the WNET Group identity in March 2021 to describe its expanding family of public media services. At that time, those services included THIRTEEN, WLIW21, WLIW-FM and ALL ARTS, alongside its New Jersey operations. Its production work reached audiences beyond its local stations through PBS programs such as *Nature*, *Great Performances*, *American Masters* and *Cyberchase*. The new name brought television, radio, online publishing and educational work under a common institutional identity. [WNET's 2021 announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/wnet-rebrands-as-the-wnet-group-media-made-possible-by-all-of-you-301256254.html).

## Building NJTV

The New Jersey arrangement began with the state's 2011 decision to end its direct operation of the New Jersey Network. WNET was selected to operate the television service under a five-year agreement, while the radio stations were assigned to other public broadcasters. The plan called for a New Jersey-based board and a nightly newscast as part of the successor service, NJTV. [Current's contemporary account](https://current.org/wp-content/uploads/archive-site/pb/pb1112njn.html).

NJTV initially worked from WNET's Manhattan facilities. It moved into leased studio space at Montclair State University in December 2011, then into the Agnes Varis Studio in downtown Newark in 2015. The Newark location brought newsroom staff together and served as the network’s studio and headquarters. [New Jersey Monthly's 2016 history](https://njmonthly.com/articles/jersey-living/letting-stories-shine-njtv/).

Its local schedule combined programs inherited from NJN, including *State of the Arts* and *Reporters Roundtable*, with newer productions such as *American Songbook at NJPAC* and *Pasta and Politics*. This programming sat alongside the nightly news and national PBS offerings. [New Jersey Monthly's programming account](https://njmonthly.com/articles/jersey-living/letting-stories-shine-njtv/).

## NJ Spotlight and the 2026 transition

In March 2019, WNET acquired NJ Spotlight from the Community Foundation of New Jersey. The announced collaboration linked the digital outlet's policy reporting with NJTV News' television coverage. The two newsrooms had already worked together on projects including the 2018 congressional debates. [WNET's acquisition announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/njtv-news-and-nj-spotlight-join-forces-as-wnet-acquires-nj-spotlight-to-increase-and-amplify-local-journalism-in-the-state-of-new-jersey-300806211.html).

Montclair became operator on July 1, 2026. PMNJ agreed to continue transitional scheduling and newscast production through September. [WNET's notice, updated July 1](https://thirteen.org/serving-new-jersey-into-the-future/). See the [operator transition](operator-transition.md) for the dated account of that handover.
