# Montclair State University and the College of Communication and Media

Montclair State University has operated [NJ PBS](nj-pbs.md) since July 1, 2026.
The New Jersey Public Broadcasting Authority retains the station licenses.
([NJPBA's operating agreement overview](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/njpba/))

The university's College of Communication and Media houses its NJ PBS work.
([College overview](https://inside.montclair.edu/colleges-schools-research/college-communication-and-media))

## University history

Montclair opened in 1908 as a normal school, an institution for preparing
teachers. It became Montclair State Teachers College in 1927, offering a
four-year degree for secondary school teachers, and received authority to
award master's degrees in 1932. As its curriculum and enrollment expanded,
it became Montclair State College in 1958. New Jersey's Board of Higher
Education granted university status on April 27, 1994.
([Montclair's institutional history](https://irdata.montclair.edu/institutionalresearch/FactBooks/1997-98/history.htm))

## College of Communication and Media

The former School of Communication and Media became a college on July 1,
2025. The college, which uses the abbreviation CCOM, has four academic
departments: Animation, Film, and Television; Communication and Media Studies;
Journalism and Sports Media; and Strategic Communication. Their programs
cover media production, reporting, communication research, advertising, and
public relations.
([College history and department descriptions](https://inside.montclair.edu/colleges-schools-research/college-communication-and-media))

Student media provide opportunities to publish and produce work in several
formats. WMSC 90.3 FM is the student radio station, *The Montclarion* is the
newspaper, and News Lab is a digital news outlet. The Red Hawk Sports Network
covers university athletics, while Hawk+ is a streaming service. The college
also supports a student communications agency and digital production work.
Its teaching facilities include television studios and a newsroom, and its
industry partnerships include NBC 4 New York, Telemundo 47, and NBCU Academy.
([College's public program and student-media guide](https://www.montclair.edu/academics/schools-colleges/college-communication-and-media))

## Center for Cooperative Media

The Center for Cooperative Media, commonly abbreviated CCM, is a program
within the college. It is distinct from the college itself. Founded in 2012,
the center supports local journalism through newsroom partnerships, training,
research, and shared reporting projects. Its NJ News Commons network helps
news organizations exchange content and work together, including independent
publishers that have limited staff and resources.
([Center's history and program description in its 2020 annual report](https://centerforcooperativemedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/CCM-annual-report-2020-final.pdf))

The center also works nationally. In February 2025, Montclair announced a
five-year, $2.5 million Knight Foundation grant for a Collaborative Media
Resource Hub. The announced goal was to support 20 journalism collaboratives
through assistance with launching partnerships and developing sustainable
operations. That grant extended the center's work studying collaboration,
convening the Collaborative Journalism Summit, and sharing research and
practical guidance with news organizations.
([Montclair's grant announcement, republished by Patch](https://patch.com/new-jersey/montclair/msu-center-cooperative-media-receives-2-5m-grant-expand-collaborative))
